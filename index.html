Process

Terms

Within 2 business days: the scope, what is left out, and a fixed price in US dollars, paid in bitcoin on-chain or over Lightning.
Review
Typically 1–3 weeks, depending on what the code does and how much of it is in scope.
Fix check
One included: after you ship fixes, I read the changed code against each finding and tell you which ones are closed.

How it works

  1. You send the repository, the commit, the parts that matter to you and your deadline.
  2. I reply with a scope and a fixed price.
  3. The review runs. The report names the commit and links every finding to a file and line, with its impact, how likely it is, a suggested fix, and what was not read. Patches with regression tests on request.
  4. You fix, I check the fixes once, and you hear which findings are closed.

Findings stay private until you have shipped a fix, or until a date we agree on before I start. After that I may publish them, and you may publish the report.

What I review

The parts of your code that can lose or leak funds, named up front:

  • entropy, seed and key generation
  • derivation and descriptors (BIP-32, 39, 86, 380s)
  • PSBT building and signing, change and address checks
  • backups, seed storage, memory and wipe paths
  • Lightning payments, swaps and channel funds
  • pool shares and payouts
  • release signing, reproducible builds, CI tokens and permissions, dependency updates

How I review

I run every review with AI models, and I read source only. Several models read the code independently and raise claims; other models then try to knock each claim down, and most don’t survive.

What’s left is traced through the code and checked against the BIP or the project’s test vectors, and anything rated medium or above is read again by a model released only to vetted security researchers.

Nothing is built or run, and the report says so. Every report goes out under my name, and I answer for it.

What sets the price

  • What the code does. Key generation and signing get read line by line; screens and settings get a lighter pass.
  • How much is in scope, counting the code that matters, not tests or vendored libraries.
  • Languages and layers. Firmware, a backend and a web frontend are three reviews in one.
  • Your deadline. A rush costs more. A report I may publish after the fix costs a little less.

What I won’t do

  • Write a pass letter, a certificate, or a sentence saying your users’ funds are safe. You get findings and a list of what was out of scope.
  • Take a seed, a private key or a live macaroon from you, for any reason.
  • Charge for a report I sent you unasked. Those are free and never come with a payment request.
